Edgefall · Hub redesign · Cutaway, second pass

The Cutaway, up close

The owner picked the Cutaway. This pass draws it as the diorama it would be, takes it from a new player to a ship fully lit, fixes the two weak points of the first pass (a dense phone and a narrow window), and settles the open choices. Nothing is built: after this, the scene goes to Blender.

The ship as a diorama

Depth
The cut faces the camera, seen a little from above and from the right. Every bay shows its floor, its back wall and one side wall; the hull shows its top and its flank. One fixed camera and no perspective to tune.
Light
One lamp per bay in its deck’s colour, a pool of it on the floor, and nothing else lit. The hull, the walls and a locked deck stay near black. The marble is the only white thing on screen.
Deck colours
A warm lamp for the Mess, cold cyan for Cargo, sodium for the Furnace, violet for the Chapel, cold white for the Bridge: the keys the fights already use (DECK_KEY).
Outside
The dome and its mast, the keel and three nozzles, the Airlock’s tube and the Mining cut’s cable down to Hollow. The moon’s lit limb is the only warm light outside the ship.

From a new player to a full ship

All five decks lit: the dense case

This was the first risk: 31 bays on one phone screen. Three things keep it readable.

  1. One loud deck. The deck under the marble is at full light. The other lit decks drop to 70 %: still readable, still one tap away. The eye lands where the marble is.
  2. Short names on a sign. Each bay wears the phone’s short name at 9 px on a dark sign over its door, so the lamp never washes it out. A second line appears only where it tells something: “seat”, the medal’s turns, the boss of the day.
  3. The bay is the button. 62 × 72 px each, wall to wall, so nothing overlaps and a tap between two rooms does not exist.

Do the labels fit?

Measured on this frame in headless Chromium with the game’s font, Chakra Petch: 31 bay names on 36 lines, the widest line 52.3 px in a sign 57 px wide, none over. Assembly Line, Unstable Core, Ballast Mind, Dual Room and Rail Yard take two lines. Magnetizer, the longest single word, is set at 8.5 px: at 9 px it measured 58.1 px, 1 px over its sign.

Today’s Hub, for comparison: labels 33 to 78 px wide and 17 px high, 8 px apart, on one deck at a time.

A window gets a wider ship

The second layout
Above 600 px wide, where labels already switch to their full names, the two big decks unfold. The Mess and Cargo lie ten bays wide, and the Furnace, the Chapel and the Bridge stand on them as a tower of six. The ship reads as a hull with its superstructure.
Width used
The ship is 820 px wide in a 1000 px window, 82 % of it; the first pass used 34 %. A bay is 76 × 88 px and wears the ship’s own name: House table, Morning count, Mining cut.
One kit, two shells
The bays, their props and their order from the lift outwards are the same in both layouts. Only the hull shell differs, and only one is loaded at a time. The notice board and the next-goal card float over the hull’s shoulders.
Smaller windows
The camera frames the whole ship, so at 960 × 540 it scales to about 0.85 and a bay is about 65 × 75 px. Under 600 px wide, or 500 px high, the tall layout takes over.

One bay, six states

The roll

  1. The tap. A tap on a bay, or on the goal line, starts the roll. The route is already drawn.
  2. The lift. The marble rolls to the lift and rides it. An open hatch lets it through; a locked one bumps it back and Tally says its line, as today.
  3. The arrival. It rolls along the floor into the bay and the room opens, after the first-entry confirm where today has one.

A step is one bay passed or one level ridden. The longest trip, from the far end of the Mess to the far end of the Bridge, is 11 steps.

Arrow keys do the same by hand: left and right along a floor, up and down in the lift.

The next goal, always in sight

Two ideas come from the other concepts: the amber beacon of the Hull Walk, and from Six Doors the rule that the centre of the screen says what to do next. In the Cutaway the centre is the marble and its lift.

In a window the next-goal card over the right shoulder takes the place of the goal line, with a Go button.

One home for every feature

Today a mode can be started from up to four places: a room of the Hub, a row of the Modes menu, a button of the dock, and a place in the Open world. The owner asked for one home each. This section gives the rule, lists every feature before and after, and shows the screens that result.

The rule

A feature starts in one place. Any other screen may show its state and lead there. It never starts it.

  • A room of the ship is in the Hub. Every mode, every boss, the run, the Daily, the records and the logs.
  • A place on Hollow is in the Open world. What is found by walking: wild fights, teleporters, the map, towns and their Workshop. A tower and a gate stay as ways in: they open the ship on their bay.
  • An action on the game in progress is in the dock. New, Hint, Undo, Replay, Retry, Goal, Flee, Hold, Lobby, and the exit in the first slot.
  • A preference or a tool is in Settings.
  • The Modes menu is removed. Nothing is left for it. World and Quick leave the dock with it, and the Hub at home has no dock at all.

Why this one

  • It leaves one list of doors, drawn as a place, where today there are two lists and a place.
  • A boss has one start, one confirm, one exit and one record, whichever way the player came.
  • The ship is the light screen: 28,125 triangles today, against 708,500 for the Open world (#1687). Replaying a boss should not load the moon.
  • It fits the story: a tower is a section of the ship (world.md section 13), so rolling into one shows that section from inside.

Fast, without walking

  • From any game: the exit, then a bay. Two taps to any mode.
  • In the ship: one tap on a bay. The marble does the walking.
  • The goal line, or Go in a window: one tap to the next goal.
  • A returning player still lands on the Hub at boot, and an invite link still opens its table.

Tower or bay: the bay stays

A boss fight starts from its bay in the ship. Its tower stays where it stands on Hollow, with what it guards and gives, and becomes the way to that bay: rolling onto it opens the ship on the boss’s deck, the door amber, the confirm already up. The same holds for a gate and the Mining cut, and the gate hands over its biome.

The price is one more tap in the world before a fight. In return there is one start, and the fight a player found by walking is the one they replay later from the ship.

The other clean rule

The reverse also gives one home: the tower and the gate start the fight and the run, and the ship’s boss bays and Mining cut become travel doors to the towers and gates already found, the fast travel openworld.md already plans. It keeps the world as built and follows Decision #1513 to the letter, and it loads the Open world for every boss replay.

This is the one real choice of this section, and it amends the door table of ui.md section 4.10. It belongs to the owners.

The screens after

Code, roughly

  • Goes: #modes, #mode-menu and its 12 mode rows with #boss-list and #coop-list, #menu-view, #quick-game, #open-world-entry, #openworld-quick, the lock banner and note, menuorder.ts, and the menu half of unlock.ts (LOCKED_IDS, paintLocks).
  • Moves: Replay to the dock; the update row and the Console to Settings; Logs and the Ledger out of Settings; the co-op boss list into the Shift crew’s sheet.
  • Changes: the plain table gets the exit in its first slot (it is no longer a home); the Hub opens while the first-win lock is on; a tower and a gate open the Hub on a bay where they start a fight or a run today (enterPlace, lairFrom, placeFrom).
  • New: a two-row sheet for the Clamp list (Ranked, Leaderboard) and for the Shift crew (with a partner, against a player).

Tests, roughly

  • 75 of the 185 end-to-end specs press the Modes sheet or one of its rows, and 41 of them start a boss through #boss-list. They need a hook that starts the mode directly, which rule 3 of AGENTS.md already asks for.
  • quick-game.spec.ts goes. door-grammar, first-win, mode-switch, back and phone-layout are rewritten.
  • 8 specs name a lair; those that roll onto a tower or a gate and expect the fight at once change, openworld-enter-places and openworld-enter-transition among them.
  • 53 unit test files name the menu, most of them main.*.test.ts that start a boss from the list. menuorder.test.ts goes; unlock, main.unlock, main.dock, sheets and settingsgroups change.

Counted with grep on selectors and button names, not by running anything.

What changes for the Blender scene

  • Two hull shells: the tall one (five bays wide) and the wide one (a hull of ten bays under a tower of six), each with its dome, mast, keel and nozzles.
  • One bay shell with depth: floor, back wall, one side wall, a lamp that takes the deck’s colour, a sign over the door.
  • Props as before: the 13 of the current deck file, new ones for the Medal wall, the Shift crew and the Logs, and five set pieces (ore hold, crates, candles, vents, the Bridge console).
  • The lift shaft, its car, a hatch per deck (open or barred), the Airlock’s tube, the Mining cut’s cable, Hollow’s limb.

Not verified

  • These are drawings. The engine’s light and glow will differ, and nothing was built in Blender or in code.
  • Label widths were measured in headless Chromium, not in the game, and only on the all-lit phone frame.
  • The first-pass cost estimate stands, about 8,000 triangles and 25 draw calls. It was not measured, and the bay’s depth adds a few faces per bay.
  • The 960 × 540 fit of the wide layout is computed, not drawn.
  • Whether a table started from an upper deck takes that deck’s board look today was not checked. If it does, that variety goes unless the table’s own sheet offers it.
  • The card of the first win is proposed wording; it was not checked against the story’s rules for Tally’s lines.
  • Today’s dock on each screen is read from thumb-dock.md, ui.md section 4.10 and index.html, not re-measured mode by mode.
  • The spec and test counts of the de-duplication come from grep on selectors and button names. Nothing was run.
  • Keyboard order, screen-reader names and the focus ring on a bay are not drawn.
